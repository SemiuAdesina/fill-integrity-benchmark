## The two root causes

**Tickets named frames by address.** `Pending` stored a raw `std::coroutine_handle<>` captured at queue time. Once the frame died and the arena handed the same block out again, that handle pointed at *another job's live frame*, so a late ticket resumed the wrong coroutine — use-after-free under ASAN. A ticket now carries `SlotId{index, epoch}` captured from the park (`FrameArena::slot_of`, while the frame is alive) plus a `bound` flag, and delivery compares **both** numbers against the frame the job is parked in *now*. A ticket queued while the job wasn't parked is bound to no frame and can never be accepted. Neither number alone is identity: `parent_two` gets index-equal/epoch-different, `parent_then_park` gets epoch-equal/index-different.

**Frames outlived their jobs.** `settle()` only set `pending_release_`, which `step()` didn't always drain, and cancellation resumed the parked frame instead of destroying it. Now `settle()` is guarded (one `JobSettled`, one `FaultReported`, and only when the outcome is Faulted) and releases storage at once — directly when settling from outside a frame (cancellation), or right after the resume returns for a root that reached `final_suspend`.

## The rest

- **Cancellation**: `CancelRequested` is recorded on every call, including unknown and already-settled ids. A parked job is *destroyed, never resumed* (destroying the root cascades through the `ChildAwaiter`s, so inner frames go first and cleanup runs). A cancel asked for a *running* job waits until control returns to the conductor, then applies at its next park; a job asked to stop never delivers a value. Repeats do nothing further.
- **Child before parent**: `ChildAwaiter::await_resume` copies the result out, destroys the child frame, *then* records `ChildDestroyed` and `ParentContinued` (with `live_slots()` at that instant), then rethrows if it faulted.
- **Parked state** is cleared on delivery, when a child starts, and when a child is destroyed — never left dangling at a dead frame.
- **Ordering/clock**: `step` picks `min(ready, job, seq)`; the clock moves to that ticket's ready time at the start of the delivery (accept or reject) and nowhere else, never backwards; delays run from the clock at queue time.
