## The fix

Only `/app/include/roundel/runtime.hpp` and `/app/src/runtime.cpp` changed. The runtime now models a job as an explicit **stack of frames owned by the conductor** (`JobState::stack`, root at the bottom), which is what makes the rest fall out:

**One outcome, one release.** `settle()` is idempotent — it ignores anything once `st->settled` is set, records `JobSettled` once (plus one `FaultReported` when the outcome is `Faulted`), and marks the job for release. `release_frames()` then destroys the stack back-to-front and records one `FrameReleased` per job. Release happens in `finish()`, which runs as soon as the outermost `resume()` returns — so it's inside the same `launch`/`step`/`cancel` call, not a step later.

**Tickets bound to (index, epoch).** A ticket binds, at queue time, to `slot_of(stack.back())` — and only if the job exists, is unsettled *and is parked*. Unbound tickets can never be accepted. At delivery both numbers are compared against the frame the job is actually parked in now, so recycled slots (same index, new epoch) and sibling frames (same epoch, different index) are both refused, and a rejection touches nothing. Re-parking in the same frame still matches, so valid late tickets arrive.

**Cancellation destroys instead of resuming.** Cancel of a parked job tears the whole stack down inner-first (so a child's frame goes before the frame that awaited it, and frame-held cleanup runs) and settles `Cancelled`. A job cancelling *itself* while running can't have its frame destroyed mid-execution, so it stops at the point it would next wait — and if it finishes first it still ends `Cancelled`. Every `cancel` call records `CancelRequested` exactly once, including for settled and never-launched ids.

**Children.** `ChildAwaiter` hands the child frame to the conductor at `await_suspend` (so the parent's frame teardown can't double-free it), and `await_resume` destroys the child *before* recording `ParentContinued` — for values and re-raised failures alike, so the arena count on `ParentContinued` is the count with the child already gone.

**Ordering and clock.** `step` picks the minimum by (ready time, job id, queue sequence), sets `tick_` to that ticket's ready time and nothing else; delays are measured from the clock at queue time.

**Also hardened:** a frame created before any conductor exists now carries a null-owner header, so `operator delete` can reliably tell pool storage from ordinary storage.

## Verification

Beyond the demo (all four scenarios now correct, `live=0`/`double_reclaims=0` throughout), I wrote four suites in `/tmp` (~429k assertions): per-rule conformance, edge cases, a randomized fuzz across capacities 1–6 with per-step invariants, and exotic cases (launch-from-inside-a-job, foreign-arena frames, nested conductors, arena exhaustion inside a job). All pass under g++ and clang++ at `-O0/-O1/-O2`, and clean under ASan/UBSan with leak detection. Note: ASan intermittently segfaults *at startup* in this sandbox — even a hello-world ASan binary fails ~30% of runs — so I re-ran until each binary produced complete, report-free passes.

Two judgment calls where the spec is silent, resolved toward the reading the spec supports: `FaultReported` is recorded only when a job settles `Faulted` (so a job that catches a failure records none), and `ChildDestroyed` is recorded only in the child-completion handshake — teardown of a cancelled job is covered by its single `FrameReleased`, which is also why rule 8's inner-first order isn't listed among the journal-ordering rules.
