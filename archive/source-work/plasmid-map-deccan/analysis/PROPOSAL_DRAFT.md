Verify a cloning construct by restriction mapping. `/app/backbone.fasta` is a real vector's complete GenBank sequence, `/app/enzymes.json` gives real recognition sites and cut offsets, and `/app/digests.json` lists the gel bands of single and double digests of the recombinant: an insert of unknown sequence, length and orientation ligated at one of the vector's listed cloning sites. Bands are integer lengths with intensity multiplicities, carry the stated sizing error, and omit fragments below the detection limit. A Rust solver writes `/app/map.json`: the recombinant's circular map (total length, insertion site, insert length, every enzyme's cut positions in vector coordinates) and predicted bands for the held-out digests named in the input.

A map is consistent when every predicted band matches an observed band of the same digest within the sizing error with equal multiplicity, undetectable fragments excepted. Among consistent maps the answer minimizes (number of insert-internal cut sites, total absolute sizing deviation, canonical key of cut positions by enzyme name then position). Graded constructs are kept only when an exact branch-and-bound oracle certifies a unique canonical optimum whose held-out digests reproduce those of the true construct.

The verifier holds the true recombinant assembled from real records, recomputes every digest from the submitted map, checks consistency and the objective, compares it with the certified optimum and replays the held-out digests against the truth; submitted scores are not authoritative. Placing each enzyme's sites from its own single digest and reconciling afterwards fails: single digests fix only circular distances, coupling and orientation appear only in double digests, and sizing error leaves many local placements consistent; only a joint exact search with bounds reaches the optimum. Every construct gets this check before sequencing; a molecular biologist comfortable with exact search needs about four hours.
