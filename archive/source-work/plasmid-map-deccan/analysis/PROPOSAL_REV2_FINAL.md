Verify a cloning construct by restriction mapping, as cloning cores do before sequencing. `/app/backbone.fasta` is a real vector; `/app/enzymes.json` gives recognition sequences, cut offsets and cloning sites; `/app/digests.json` gives per digest (an enzyme set) observed bands (length, multiplicity), sizing error e%, detection limit D, and bounds: insert length in [Lmin,Lmax], at most K internal sites per enzyme. The recombinant is the vector opened at cloning site c with L unknown bases inserted (positions from c shift by L; orientation is implicit in the internal offsets). A Rust solver writes `/app/map.json`: c, L, each enzyme's cut positions on the circle of length V+L (vector-region positions must match the sequence) and predicted bands (≥ D, with multiplicities) for the named held-out digests.

A digest's cuts are the union of its enzymes' positions (coincident cuts merge); fragments are the circular gaps (uncut: one band, V+L); those below D are unseen. A digest matches when predicted fragments ≥ D and observed bands, expanded by multiplicity and sorted, pair off with equal counts, each within ceil(e·observed/100); deviation is the summed absolute error. Among maps matching every observed digest the answer is the lexicographic minimum of (internal sites, deviation, sorted (enzyme, position) list). Graded constructs are kept only if an exact oracle certifies a unique optimum whose held-out bands match the true construct.

The verifier holds the true recombinant from real records; it validates the map physically, recomputes every digest, checks matching and objective against the certified optimum and replays held-out digests; submitted scores are not authoritative. Instances: 2.5-5 kb vectors, 3-5 enzymes, ≤3 internal sites each, inserts to 6 kb, all singles and 3-6 doubles observed, 2 held out; singles fix only circular distances, doubles couple enzymes and orientation under sizing error. A molecular biologist fluent in exact search: about four hours.
